# The Draw — INTENT

**What this file is.** The "why this exists" doc. See [STATUS.md](STATUS.md) for current state and `../_docs/THE-DRAW-VISION.md` for the full vision and mechanics.

---

## Mission

The Draw is a writing practice built on a single constraint: 10 words drawn at random from a hand-curated pool of 1,290, turned into one published piece each week. The constraint is not gimmick; it is the engine. The randomness removes the blank page problem. The words resist laziness. The cadence exists to build the habit.

The writing comes first. The website, the email list, and the archive exist to serve the practice and, over time, to let it reach people.

---

## The constraint, plainly

- 1,290 words in the pool. 10 drawn per week. 129 weeks of runway (~2.5 years).
- Once a word is drawn, it's used. The pool is finite on purpose — the project has a shape.
- All 10 words must appear in the piece. Honestly, not decoratively.
- Format follows the words: essay, poem, letter, fragment — whatever the words demand.

---

## The operating rule now

The project has been stalled not because the idea is weak, but because the weekly unit of work was too emotionally heavy. The Draw now runs as a small weekday production rhythm, not as one vague "write a good newsletter" obligation.

- Monday: generate or confirm the week's 10 words.
- Tuesday: write for 25 minutes and produce a rough draft.
- Wednesday: revise lightly and publish that week's piece.
- Thursday: record what shipped or what blocked publication.
- Friday: catch up only if the Wednesday publish was missed.

The week's success condition is simple: one piece gets published. The piece can be short. It does not need to feel major.

---

## Fallback rule

Missed days should not create an expanding guilt backlog.

- If Monday is missed, do the draw before Tuesday's writing block.
- If Tuesday is missed, Wednesday becomes draft plus publish if possible.
- If Wednesday is missed, Thursday becomes forced publish day.
- If the week is missed entirely, skip the debt and start fresh with the next week's draw.

Never carry more than one missed issue mentally. The project survives by keeping motion cheap.

---

## What the infrastructure does (and doesn't do)

The site is not the project. The site is what lets the project persist and be shared. `draw.py` handles the pull. `publish.py` handles the handoff to Vercel. Buttondown handles the list. None of this matters if nothing gets written.

The measure of the project is pieces written, not tools built.

---

## What it is not

- Not a word-of-the-day app or vocabulary tool.
- Not primarily a newsletter (the list is a side effect of the writing, not the goal).
- Not something that needs more features. The infrastructure is complete.
- Not a perfection test. Reliability beats ambition here.

---

## Success looks like

- One piece is published each week, even if it is brief.
- The morning brief can name the single required Draw action for that day.
- The habit takes hold — 10 words drawn, something written, published.
- By Week 6, the cadence feels automatic enough that the project no longer depends on willpower.
- By Week 52, there's an archive worth reading.
